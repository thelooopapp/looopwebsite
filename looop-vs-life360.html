Looop vs. Life360

Life360 shows you where everyone is. Looop tells you when to check in.

Looking for a Life360 alternative? Looop is a safety notifications app for families who care from a distance. It stays quiet when all is well and sends a calm heads-up when it might be a good time to call. It never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers.

Here's an honest, sourced look at how the two compare, including where Life360 may be the better fit.

"So what's actually different?"

The differences that matter

Same worry. Very different answers.

  • Built for families, not for upgrades

    Looop was designed from the first screen around one question: "Is Mom okay today?" Every alert exists to answer it.

    Life360 is adding aging parents to an app built for a different job. Its aging-parent page pitches its premium Gold and Platinum plans. On its Q2 2026 earnings call, CEO Lauren Antonoff named aging parents as a next line of business, alongside advertising and pets. Its head of design credits his redesign with a 22% lift in premium conversion. Those are fair goals for a public company. They're just not the same goal as ours.

  • Your parent's data isn't ad inventory

    Looop never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers. There are no ads and no sponsored offers in your notifications.

    Life360's ad business is growing fast. It reported $22 million in ad revenue in Q2 2026, up 315% from a year earlier. Members who land at an airport can get an Uber ride offer in their landing notification, and its Place Ads reach families based on the places they visit. Life360 also has a history of selling location data to a dozen data brokers, and still sells location data today.

  • A storm near Mom is a heads-up, not an ad slot

    When severe weather heads toward a Loved One, Looop tells you, in the US and worldwide. That's the whole alert. It might be a good time to call.

    Life360 offers free severe weather alerts too, through AccuWeather. In the same announcement, it said it will use AccuWeather data to target ads during weather events, such as a retailer promoting emergency supplies or repair services to members in affected areas.

  • Crime near Mom, not a crime map

    Looop sends a calm heads-up when crime is reported near a Loved One, so you can reach out if it feels right. You pick the distance: 1, 3, 5 or 10 miles. Crime alerts work in supported cities, more than 200 US cities and counties so far, so check whether your family's towns are covered.

    We didn't find crime alerts mentioned in Life360's App Store listing when we checked in October 2026.

  • A nudge before the scam, not a refund after

    If you link a bank account, Looop can let you know about unusually large spending, with a threshold you set. A sudden large payment can be an early sign of a scam, so you can call Mom that same day. Bank linking is optional, runs through Plaid, and is never sold.

    Life360's premium plans include identity theft protection and stolen-funds reimbursement, which help after a loss. At a Goldman Sachs conference in September 2026, it said it is working on scam protection for aging parents.

  • One job, not a super app

    Looop does one thing: tell you when to check in. It uses only what it needs to tell whether something unusual is going on.

    Life360 is aiming wider. In a May 2026 post, Executive Chairman Chris Hulls describes a family super app with calendar events, bills, RSVPs and a drawer for documents like insurance cards. He calls the approach "aggressive on permissions, anchored in trust." Some families will love that. Mom may just want fewer worried calls.

  • Built for people, not pets and keys

    Looop is only for people, around their routine and their independence. Mom keeps her day. You get a call-worthy heads-up when it counts.

    Life360 brings people, pets and things into one app. Its App Store listing includes Tile trackers for items and a Pet GPS, and in August 2026 it added new pet products, including a Pet Tag and activity alerts for dogs. That's handy for a busy household. Mom doesn't need a collar. She needs a call when it counts.

  • No extra watch app needed

    If your Loved One wears an Apple Watch and chooses to share wellness, Looop reads that data through Apple Health on their iPhone. Nothing new to install on the watch.

    Life360 solves a different problem. It released a separate Life360 for Watch app in June 2026 for people who wear a watch without carrying a phone, and described it as in beta on its Q2 2026 call.

"Is Looop right for us?"

Where Life360 may fit better

We'd rather you pick the right app than the wrong one.

Android families

Looop is iPhone only today. Life360 works on iPhone and Android, so a mixed-phone family can all join.

24/7 dispatch and roadside help

Life360's Gold and Platinum plans include emergency dispatch and roadside assistance, and paid plans include crash detection. Looop has none of these. It offers 911 and emergency contacts in one tap.

A live map of young kids

If you want to see where young children are throughout the day, with arrival and departure alerts and location history, Life360 is built for that.

Pets and keys in one place

If you want your dog's GPS and your Tile item trackers in the same app as your family, Life360 offers that. Looop is only for people.

Questions families ask

Can I switch from Life360 to Looop?

Yes. Download Looop, sign up with your phone number, and invite your Loved Ones from your contacts. They join with a simple six-character code. Before you send invites, talk it through together and show everyone what Looop shares. If you pay for a Life360 plan, remember to cancel it the way you signed up.

Does Looop sell my data?

No. Looop never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers. There are no ads in the app and no sponsored offers in your notifications. You can delete your account and data whenever you choose.

Does Looop work on Android?

Not today. Looop is available for iPhone on the App Store. If your family includes Android phones, Life360 may be a better fit for now.

Does Looop have crash detection or emergency dispatch?

No. Looop doesn't have crash detection, a 24/7 dispatch service or roadside help. It lets you call 911 in one tap and save up to three emergency contacts for each Loved One. Looop helps you know when to check in. It doesn't detect emergencies. If you think someone is in danger, call 911 first.

Does Looop have a map?

Yes, as a supporting feature. After a nudge, the family map is there to help you decide what to do next. Looop is built around notifications, so you don't need to keep checking it.

Will my mom know she's in our Looop?

Yes. Every Loved One joins by invitation. Wellness sharing, like steps, heart rate and sleep, is optional and always their call.

Where do crime alerts work?

In supported cities: more than 200 US cities and counties so far. Severe weather alerts work in the US and worldwide.
