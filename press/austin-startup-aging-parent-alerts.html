Austin Startup Already Ships Aging-Parent Alerts While a Public Company Is Still Promising Them

Draft for review·Placeholders in brackets are not final

Looop sends alerts about a parent's health, fitness, spending, and nearby crime, brought together in one daily answer for the family

AUSTIN, Texas, Oct. 27, 2026: LooopApp LLC, an Austin startup, today announced that Looop sends the kind of aging-parent alerts a public company is still promising. Families get alerts about a parent's health, fitness, and unusual spending, along with severe weather, nearby crime, and changes in her routine, brought together in one daily answer. Looop is a safety notifications app for families who care from a distance, built by an independent startup that doesn't work with advertisers.

If Mom's sleep looks off, a large charge hits her account, or her phone sits untouched for hours, the family gets a heads-up, so someone can call.

In October 2025, Life360 CEO Lauren Antonoff discussed on Fox Business how the company is exploring features for aging parents. On its Q4 2025 earnings call in March 2026, the company said it was working on devices for aging parents but did not expect to launch them that year. On its Q2 2026 call in August, it named aging parents as a next line of business. And at a Goldman Sachs conference in September, it said it is working on services for aging parents, including wellness features, scam protection, and emergency response.

In 2026, Life360 also added Safety Incidents, a layer on its map that members can turn on or off. It shows recent, high-severity incidents within one mile as pins and sends a notification.

Looop's aging-parent alerts are already here. Looop launched on the App Store in May 2026 and has shipped new features and updates steadily since.

Looop tells families when Mom's day looks different.
That's when a call is worth making.

Looop takes a holistic approach to family care: health, fitness, spending, activity, weather, crime, and the shape of her day, in one place. Families don't have to piece together how Mom is doing from a health app, a banking app, a weather app, and a location app.

  • Her health: heart rate, breathing, and sleep, through Apple Health and devices like Apple Watch, Oura, and Whoop.
  • How she's moving: steps, walks, and workouts, from the same devices.
  • Any unusual spending: a heads-up about an unusually large charge on a linked account, which can be an early sign of a scam.
  • What's changed: walking, driving, time spent in one place, phone use that's higher or lower than usual, and a phone left untouched for hours.
  • What's around her: severe weather in more than 50 countries and nearby crime in all 50 US states.
  • Where she is: a heads-up when she arrives at a hospital or ER, a police station, or an airport, and a map for when you need it.

Each day, all of it comes together in one answer: a Typical Day, or an Unusual Day that might be worth a call.

Every loved one joins Looop by invitation and decides whether to add Health & Fitness sharing or link a bank account.

"We built Looop because our own families are spread across the country and around the world. We're Gen X, looking after kids in college and aging parents at the same time. We don't want to track them. We want them to keep their independence, and we want to know when it's time to call, so we're ready to help," said the team behind Looop.

Private by design

Looop never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers. Its notifications carry no ads or sponsored offers.

About Looop

Looop is a safety notifications app that helps families know their loved ones are safe, across any distance. It brings holistic family care to the people you love: safety alerts instead of tracking, where health, fitness, spending, activity, weather, crime, and the shape of each day come together in one answer, so families know when to check in and loved ones keep their independence. Looop has no ads, ever, and your family's data is never for sale. Learn more at https://www.thelooopapp.com.
