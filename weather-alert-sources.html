"Who says there's a storm near Dad?"

Official sources, passed along

Each alert comes from the agency that issued it.

Looop passes on alerts from official agencies and shows data from trusted providers. It doesn't make its own forecasts or warnings.

  • Alerts come from the people in charge. Severe weather and emergency alerts come from national weather services and state emergency agencies. Looop checks them for each Loved One's location and lets you know when one applies.
  • The full alert is one tap away. Alerts in Looop link back to the issuing agency, so you can read their own instructions and the latest updates.
  • Alerts depend on where they are. Current conditions and forecasts work worldwide. Severe weather and emergency alerts work only where one of the sources below covers the area. See where alerts work.

Looop isn't an emergency service and doesn't replace official warnings. Always follow the advice of local authorities. If you think someone is in danger, call your local emergency number.

Where alerts work

Severe weather alerts in more than 50 countries.
Today's coverage, by place.

  • United States: severe weather alerts from the National Weather Service.
  • Canada, Mexico, Europe, and Israel: severe weather alerts through Apple Weather, from each country's weather service.
  • Australia: weather warnings from the Bureau of Meteorology through Xweather, plus bushfire and emergency warnings in New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, and Western Australia.
  • New Zealand: severe weather watches and warnings from MetService.
  • Philippines: weather alerts from PAGASA.
  • Brazil: weather warnings from INMET.
  • Japan, South Korea, India, and South Africa: severe weather alerts through Xweather, from each country's weather service.
  • China, Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan, the UAE, and Singapore: severe weather alerts through OpenWeather, from national weather services.

Current conditions and forecasts work everywhere. Coverage may change as sources are added or removed.

"Where does the forecast come from?"

Weather and location

Current conditions, forecasts, and place names.

  • Open-Meteo

    Current conditions and five-day forecasts for every Loved One, worldwide. Looop shows this data in its own design and units.

    Weather data by Open-Meteo.com. CC BY 4.0.

  • NASA FIRMS

    Satellite fire detections near each loved one, shown as a "Possible Fire Nearby" card in the weather tab with a link to NASA's fire map. These cards never send a notification, because satellites also see controlled burns and industrial heat.

    We acknowledge the use of data from NASA's Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS), part of NASA's Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS). firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov

  • BigDataCloud

    Finds the country and city for a location outside the US, so Looop knows which alert source to check and which city name to show.

    Reverse geocoding by BigDataCloud. Terms.
