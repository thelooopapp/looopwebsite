# The Best Apps to Check On an Aging Parent in 2026

"Is Mom okay today?"

Maybe she lives a few states away and still drives to church on Sundays. Maybe Dad lives alone in the house you grew up in. You don't want to call every morning, and they don't want you to. You just want to know when a call is worth making.

Plenty of apps say they can help, and they do different jobs. Some show where a parent is, and others get help in an emergency. Our own app, Looop, tells you when it might be time to check in. Many families end up using two. We'll tell you who each one fits, including when the answer isn't Looop.

*Facts about each app come from its App Store listing, help pages, and policies, linked in the sources below. Checked October 26, 2026. Prices and features change, so check each one before you decide.*

## Start with the question you're really asking

Each kind of app answers a different worry:

- **"Where is she?"** A location sharing app: Apple Find My, Life360, iSharing, or GeoZilla.
- **"Did she get there okay?"** A check-in for one trip: Apple's Check In.
- **"Who sends help if something happens?"** An emergency service: OnStar Guardian for a parent who drives, a medical alert service, or Life360's dispatch on its top plans.
- **"Is she okay today?"** A safety notifications app: Looop.

## Apple's built-in tools: Find My, Check In, and Health Sharing

If your parent has an iPhone, start here. These tools are free and already on her phone.

### Find My

[Find My](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/find-my/id1514844621) lets your parent share her location with you for a set time or until she chooses to stop. You can [get notified](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/get-notified-when-a-friends-location-changes-iph843dd79b6/ios) when she arrives at or leaves a place, like home or the grocery store. It also finds a lost iPhone, AirPods, or keys with an AirTag, and on iPhone 14 and later it can share location over satellite. Its App Store label has no "Data Used to Track You" section.

### Check In

According to [Apple's guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/use-check-in-for-messages-ips56b5bc469/web), Check In, in Messages, is built for one trip or one evening: your parent starts it with a timer or a destination, and if she doesn't respond when it's time, it shares her location, battery, and signal with you. It's a good fit for a drive to see a friend or an evening out. Each check-in starts with her.

### Health Sharing

Apple says a parent can share Health data, and notifications about trends, with family in its Health app. It's a good fit if your parent wants you to see the readings she's already collecting.

**Best for:** iPhone families who want free, simple tools and don't mind each one living in a separate place. Looop works alongside Find My.

## Life360

Life360 is one of the best-known family apps, and it now has a [page for aging parents](https://www.life360.com/aging-parents). It offers location sharing, place alerts, no-show alerts, severe weather alerts, crash detection, emergency dispatch, driving summaries, credit monitoring, and identity theft reimbursement. Many of those features need paid plans, listed from $9.99 to $24.99 on the App Store. It works on iPhone and Android.

It keeps adding more. Its 2026 [Safety Incidents](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/38226266501911-Safety-Incidents) feature puts recent, high-severity events within one mile on the map. Its [Subscription Tracker](https://www.life360.com/blog/subscription-tracker) links bank accounts through Plaid to show recurring charges. Its [setup page](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/39739802477591-Set-Up-Subscription-Tracker) says that by connecting, you agree that "Life360 may use your transaction data to show you relevant ads and measure ad performance in the Life360 app and elsewhere." Life360 runs a [growing ad business](https://investors.life360.com/static-files/e6bd1845-9bc7-429b-aada-fd260b7fc517) and has a history of selling location data to a dozen data brokers.

**Best for:** a parent and family who want a live map and 24/7 emergency dispatch, especially if some of you use Android phones.

**Choose Life360 if** you want to pay more for precision GPS tracking, driving details recorded and stored, call center support, targeted ads, and your family's data being shared with its partners.

## iSharing

iSharing is a family map with place alerts, location history, and a panic or SOS alert. It also pitches families with older parents: its "Elderly Care" alerts let you know when someone hasn't used their phone, on Silver plans and up. Premium plans are listed from $7.99 a month or $69.99 a year on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id416436167), with a seven-day trial.

Its [privacy policy](https://isharingsoft.com/privacy_policy), last revised May 21, 2026, says: "We may also share location information with third parties for advertising, research, and analytic purposes."

**Best for:** families who want a map with an inactivity alert, at a lower monthly price than Life360's top plans.

## GeoZilla

[GeoZilla](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id981856216) is a family map with arrival and departure alerts, daily location history, check-ins, chat, and an Emergency Alert that lets family know someone needs help. It shows speeding and phone use while driving, and it has an Apple Watch app. Its App Store description mentions older loved ones as well as teenagers. Plans run weekly, monthly, quarterly, or yearly, from $5.99 a week to $69.99 a year.

**Best for:** families who want one shared map for teens and grandparents alike, with an Apple Watch app.

## OnStar Guardian, for parents who still drive

If Dad still drives to golf every Tuesday, [OnStar Guardian](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1480411487) is worth a look. It works in any vehicle in the US and Canada, on iPhone. Mobile Crash Response connects to an OnStar advisor, an Emergency button reaches 24/7 advisors who can send first responders, and it includes roadside help and location with arrival alerts. It costs $15 a month on its own, or it's included for OnStar members, and it can be shared with up to seven people.

Its App Store label declares "Data Used to Track You" for usage data.

Help in an emergency is one thing.  
Knowing when to call, before there is one, is another.

OnStar Guardian and Looop fit together.

**Best for:** a parent who drives, whose family wants someone who can send help after a crash.

## Medical alert services

Medical alert services offer emergency response for older adults, and some add daily check-ins. If your parent wants a direct way to reach help, they're worth comparing. Ask each one what it costs each month, what's included, and how it lets you know when something happens.

Looop isn't an emergency service, and it doesn't replace one.

**Best for:** a parent who wants a direct way to reach help in an emergency.

## Looop

Looop is our app, so read this section with that in mind. It's a safety notifications app for families who care from a distance. Most family apps are built around a map: they answer "Where is she?"  
Looop answers "Is Mom okay today?"

Looop takes a holistic approach to family care: weather, crime, spending, health, fitness, activity, and the shape of her day. You get a heads-up when severe weather heads toward Mom, in more than 50 countries, and when crime is reported near her, in all 50 US states, at 1, 3, 5, or 10 miles. If driving, walking, or phone activity looks unusual, Looop sends you a nudge.

Spending alerts are optional: a heads-up about an unusually large charge on a linked account, which can be an early sign of a scam. Health & Fitness sharing through Apple Health is available if you need it.

Each day, all of it comes together in one answer: a Typical Day, or an Unusual Day that might be worth a call. Mom joins by invitation, and there's nothing for her to start each day.

Looop has no ads, ever, and your family's data is never for sale. Looop's App Store privacy label has no "Data Used to Track You" section. It's iPhone only, and it isn't an emergency service. It doesn't send help or detect crashes, though 911 and up to three emergency contacts for each Loved One are one tap away.

**Best for:** grown kids who care for a parent from a distance and want to know when to call, without calling every day.

**Choose Looop if** you want safety alerts instead of tracking, a nudge when a drive looks unusual, no ads ever, and your family's data never for sale. Peace of mind for you, independence for them.

## Which one is right for your family?

- **Your parent has an iPhone and you want free, simple sharing:** Apple Find My, with Check In for trips.
- **You want a live map and 24/7 emergency dispatch, on iPhone or Android:** Life360.
- **You want a map with an inactivity alert:** iSharing.
- **One map for teens and grandparents, with a watch app:** GeoZilla.
- **Your parent still drives, and you want help after a crash:** OnStar Guardian.
- **Your parent wants a direct way to reach help:** a medical alert service.
- **You want to know when to call, with weather, crime, and the shape of her day in one place:** Looop, alongside any of the above.

Many families combine two.  
Find My shows where she is, and Looop tells you when to call.  
OnStar Guardian covers the drive, and Looop covers the rest of the day.
