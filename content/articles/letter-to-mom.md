# A Letter to Mom: Why I Asked You to Join Looop

"How do I ask Mom to join without making her feel watched?"

Sending a parent an app invite can feel strangely hard. You know why you're asking. You love them, you live far away, and you worry. But a link with no context can land the wrong way. It can sound like "I don't trust you," when what you mean is "I miss you, and I want to know when to call."

So we wrote a letter. It's the note we'd want to send our own parents before an invite ever arrives. Copy it, change every word that doesn't sound like you, and send it by email, text or even on paper.

## Before you send it: a note for you

A few things make this letter work.

**Send it before the invite, not with it.** Give your parent time to read, think and ask questions. The invite can wait a day.

**Make it yours.** Swap in real details: their Tuesday golf game, their book club, the storm that rattled you last winter. A letter with your own memories in it will mean more than a perfect one.

**Mean the promises.** The letter makes promises in both directions. Only send the ones you'll keep.

**Be ready for "no," or "not yet."** If your parent isn't comfortable, that's a real answer. Ask what would help, and try again another time, or not at all. A tool a parent agreed to is one they'll keep.

**Talk after.** A letter opens the door. A phone call or a visit is where you walk through it together.

## The letter

Dear Mom,

I've been meaning to write this for a while, and I wanted to put it in a letter instead of rushing through it on the phone.

First, the most important part: I don't think you need looking after. You've built a full life, and you run it better than I run mine. Your lunches, your garden, your Sunday routine. I love that you live your way, and I want it to stay that way.

The honest truth is that this is about me. Living far away, I worry more than I'd like to admit. When I can't reach you for a day, my mind goes places it shouldn't. When I see a storm on the news near your town, I find out after it's passed. And I hate that my worry turns into "just checking" calls that interrupt your day.

So I'd like to ask you something, and it's completely your choice.

There's an app called Looop. It isn't about watching where you go. It stays quiet on normal days, which I hope is almost every day. It lets me know if severe weather is heading your way. And if your day looks really different from your usual, it gives me a gentle nudge that it might be a good time to call. That's it. A heads-up, not a report.

There's a map in it too, for if I ever need it. But that isn't why I'm asking. I'm asking so I'll know when a call actually matters, and leave you alone when it doesn't.

Here's what I promise you:

- **You decide.** You can say yes, no, or not yet. Any answer is okay with me.
- **No surprises.** Before you join, I'll sit with you and show you exactly what I'd see.
- **Health stays yours.** Things like steps or sleep are only shared if you turn them on. You can skip all of it.
- **You can leave anytime.** If it ever feels wrong, you can stop, and I won't make it a thing.
- **I won't quiz you.** If I call after a nudge, it'll be "thinking of you," not twenty questions.
- **Fewer check-up calls.** More calls about the grandkids, the garden and the game.
- **Your information isn't for sale.** Looop never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers. I checked.

And here's what I'd love from you, if you're willing: give it a try for a month, and then tell me honestly how it feels. If you'd like, I'll share mine with you too, so you'll know when a storm is heading toward me. We can look out for each other.

You took care of me for a long time. I'm not trying to switch places. I just want to be close, even from far away.

Let's talk about it this weekend. No pressure, I promise.

All my love,
*Your name*

## After you send it

Give it a day or two. Then call, and let your parent lead.

They might have questions. Common ones:

**"So you'll see where I am all day?"** That isn't the point. Looop is built to tell you when to check in, not to watch where they go. Show them what you'd actually see.

**"What about my health stuff?"** Optional, always. They choose each category, and they can change their mind in their iPhone's Health settings anytime.

**"What if I don't like it?"** They can leave anytime. Say so again.

**"Who else sees it?"** Only the family members in your Looop group, the people they agree to.

If they say yes, set it up side by side, ideally in person. Let them tap the buttons. If they say not yet, thank them for thinking about it. You've still told them something that matters: that you love them, and you trust them to run their own life.

## Making it work for Dad, a grandparent, or a friend

The letter works for anyone you care about from a distance. A few easy swaps:

- **For Dad:** "Your golf Tuesdays," "your workshop," "the drive to the lake."
- **For a grandparent:** Keep it short. Add a line about a favorite memory.
- **For an aunt, sibling or friend:** Lean on "look out for each other." Make it mutual from the first line.

Whoever it's for, keep the heart of it: you trust them, you worry anyway, and you'd like a quieter way to know when to call.
