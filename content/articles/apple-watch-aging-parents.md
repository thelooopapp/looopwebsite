# Using Apple Watch to Stay Close to Aging Parents, No Extra App Needed

"Dad wears his Apple Watch every day. Can that help me know when to call?"

Maybe. If your parent already wears an Apple Watch and carries an iPhone, a lot of what their watch notices is already being saved, privately, on their phone. With their permission, a little of it can help you know when a day looks different from their usual.

This guide explains how that works with Looop, what stays in your parent's hands, and how to set it up together on your next visit or call.

## The short version

- Looop doesn't need its own Apple Watch app. It reads optional wellness data through Apple Health on your parent's iPhone.
- Sharing is always optional and always your parent's choice, one category at a time.
- Looop compares each day to your parent's own normal, never to anyone else.
- It's a nudge to check in, not a medical tool. It doesn't diagnose anything or detect medical events.
- Set it up together, side by side, so nothing is a surprise.

## How it works: watch to iPhone to Looop

Here's the path, step by step.

**1. The watch notices.** As your parent wears their Apple Watch, it measures things like heart rate, sleep and steps. Apple's [sleep guide for Apple Watch](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108906) explains that sleep data, including time in REM, Core and Deep sleep, shows up in the Health app on iPhone.

**2. Apple Health keeps it on the iPhone.** The Health app is where Apple stores that data. According to [Apple's Health app privacy overview](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/health-app/), other apps must ask permission to read it, must explain why, and you can change what you share at any time.

**3. Looop reads only what your parent allows.** If your parent says yes, Looop reads the categories they turned on. Looop only reads from Apple Health. It doesn't write anything back.

**4. You see a calm summary.** Each stat is marked Typical or Unusual, based on your parent's own patterns. If something stands out, Looop gives you a gentle nudge that it might be a good time to check in.

That's why there's no extra watch app to install, update or charge. If your parent already wears the watch and carries the phone, the pieces are in place.

## What your parent can choose to share

Every one of these is optional. Your parent can share all of them, some of them, or none.

| What they can share | Where it comes from | What you might see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heart rate | Apple Watch, through Apple Health | A nudge if it's unusually high or low for them |
| Breathing rate | Apple Watch, through Apple Health | A nudge if it's unusual for them |
| Sleep | Apple Watch, through Apple Health | Deep, REM and light sleep, plus naps, marked Typical or Unusual |
| Steps and walking | iPhone and Apple Watch | Today's steps and walking time compared to their usual |
| Loud environments | Apple Watch Noise app, through Apple Health | A heads-up if they're somewhere dangerously loud |

A note on loud places: Apple's guide to the [Noise app](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102315) says Apple Watch measures sound levels using the microphone, but doesn't record or save any sounds.

## What it isn't

This part matters, so we'll say it plainly.

Looop isn't a medical device. It doesn't diagnose anything, and it doesn't detect medical events or emergencies. A nudge about an unusual heart rate or a short night's sleep means one thing: today looks different from their usual, so a call might be nice.

Lots of ordinary things can change a day's numbers. A brisk walk, a late movie, a cold, a long flight. Often the best response is a simple "Hey, just thinking of you." If you have real concerns about a parent's health, talk with them about seeing their doctor. And if you ever believe someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

## Consent first, always

Health details are personal. Even more personal than location. So the order of operations matters.

**Talk before you tap.** Explain what you're hoping for: fewer worried calls, and a way to know when a call actually matters. Lead with your feelings, not their limits. "I worry when I can't reach you" lands better than "You should be wearing this."

**Show, don't tell.** Sit together and look at what Looop would show you. Let your parent see their own Typical and Unusual labels first.

**Make every category a real choice.** Some parents are happy to share sleep but not heart rate. Some share nothing at all. All of those are good answers.

**Agree on a way out.** Your parent can change their mind any time. A parent who knows they can say no is more likely to say yes.

## How to set it up together

Plan about 15 minutes, ideally in person. A video call works too.

**1. Check the basics.** Make sure your parent's Apple Watch is paired with their iPhone and that they're wearing it most days. For sleep, they'll need to wear it to bed with Sleep turned on, per [Apple's sleep guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108906).

**2. Accept the Looop invite.** Your parent installs Looop on their iPhone and joins your group from the invitation you sent, with a simple six-character code.

**3. Walk through setup side by side.** Looop's guided setup explains each permission before iPhone asks for it. Let your parent tap the buttons.

**4. Choose Health categories together.** When iPhone shows Apple's Health permission screen, your parent picks exactly which categories Looop can read. Turn on only what they're comfortable with. Skipping this step entirely is fine too.

**5. Show them how to change it later.** Apple's guide to [managing Health data](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108779) shows the path: open the Health app, tap the profile picture, then under Privacy tap Apps. From there, your parent can turn any category on or off for any app, including Looop.

**6. Agree on what a nudge means.** Decide together what happens if Looop sends one. Maybe it's a quick text. Maybe it's a call. Maybe siblings take turns. Agreeing now means no one panics later.

## A two-sided promise

The point isn't to know more about your parent's body. It's to bother them less.

**They get** their routine and their privacy, with control over every category. Fewer "just checking on you" calls. A watch they already love, doing a little more for the people who love them.

**You get** a quiet signal on the day a call might matter, and silence on the days it doesn't. No news is good news, and you can actually believe it.

If your parent decides health sharing isn't for them, Looop still works. You'll still get a heads-up about severe weather heading their way, crime reported nearby in supported cities, and a gentle nudge when their day looks different from their usual routine.
