## What each app does well, and where it may fit better

**Question:** "What does each app do well?"

### What Life360 does well

Life360 is a family map app for both iPhone and Android, built around a live map with location history, place alerts, and driving reports. Paid plans add crash detection, and Gold and Platinum include SOS with 24/7 emergency dispatch and roadside help.

It has added more than location lately. Severe weather alerts come through AccuWeather. Its [Safety Incidents](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/38226266501911-Safety-Incidents) feature, new in 2026, puts recent, high-severity events within one mile on the map, with a notification. Its free [Subscription Tracker](https://www.life360.com/blog/subscription-tracker) links bank accounts through Plaid to show recurring charges across a Circle.

On data, Life360 runs a [growing ad business](https://investors.life360.com/static-files/e6bd1845-9bc7-429b-aada-fd260b7fc517). Its [setup page](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/39739802477591-Set-Up-Subscription-Tracker) says that by connecting an account to Subscription Tracker, you agree that "Life360 may use your transaction data to show you relevant ads and measure ad performance in the Life360 app and elsewhere." Life360 has a history of selling location data to a dozen data brokers.

**Where Life360 may fit better:** families with Android phones (Find My is for Apple devices), a household with a new teen driver, or anyone who wants crash detection on paid plans and 24/7 emergency dispatch on Gold and Platinum.

**Choose Life360 if** you want to pay more for precision GPS tracking, driving details recorded and stored, call center support, targeted ads, and your family's data being shared with its partners.

### What Find My does well

If everyone in your family has an iPhone, you already have [Find My](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/find-my/id1514844621). It's free and built in. You can share your location with friends and family for a set time or until you choose to stop, and Find My can [notify you](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/get-notified-when-a-friends-location-changes-iph843dd79b6/ios) when someone arrives at or leaves a place. It also finds Apple devices and AirTags, lets you share an item with up to five people, and can share location over satellite on iPhone 14 and later.

Apple has other tools in separate places. According to [Apple's guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/use-check-in-for-messages-ips56b5bc469/web), Check In, in Messages, lets someone start a check-in with a timer or a destination, and if they don't respond, it shares their location, battery, and signal with the person they chose. Apple says Health Sharing, in the Health app, lets a person share Health data with family.

Find My's App Store label has no "Data Used to Track You" section.

**Where Find My may fit better:** all-iPhone families who want free, simple location sharing and help finding devices and items. Looop works alongside Find My.

### Looop: Best alongside Find My, for knowing when to call

Looop is ours, so keep that in mind as you read. It's a safety notifications app from LooopApp LLC, an independent Austin startup that launched in May 2026.

Looop takes a holistic approach to family care: weather, crime, spending, health, fitness, activity, and the shape of each day. You get a heads-up when severe weather heads toward a Loved One, in more than 50 countries, and when crime is reported nearby, in all 50 US states, at 1, 3, 5, or 10 miles. If driving, walking, or phone activity looks unusual, you get a gentle nudge. Spending alerts and Health & Fitness sharing through Apple Health are available if you need them.

Looop works alongside Find My. Apple's tools live in separate places, and Looop brings it all together in one place, with nothing for Mom to start each day.

Each day, all of it comes together in one answer: a Typical Day, or an Unusual Day that might be worth a call. Looop has its own family map too, for after a nudge to check in.

Looop has no ads, ever, and your family's data is never for sale. It's iPhone only, and it isn't an emergency service. It doesn't send help or detect crashes, though 911 and up to three emergency contacts for each Loved One are one tap away.

**Choose Looop if** you want safety alerts instead of tracking, a nudge when a drive looks unusual, no ads ever, and your family's data never for sale. Peace of mind for you, independence for them.

## What Looop adds to Find My

**Question:** "What does Looop add to Find My?"
**Subtitle:** Looop is built to tell you how her day is going.

### All of it, in one answer each day

Instead of checking the weather in five cities, families get one heads-up when it matters.

Weather, crime, spending, health, fitness, activity, and the shape of each day come together in one answer, alongside the Find My you already use.

### A quiet app on a good day

When Mom's day looks typical, Looop stays quiet. When something looks off, you get a heads-up, and she doesn't get a "where are you?" text every evening.

### For a parent who lives on her own

Looop is made for a parent a few states away, kids at college, or a sibling or friend who lives alone. Loved Ones join by invitation and keep their routine. Mom doesn't have to start anything or set a timer.

### No ads in Looop, ever

Looop never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers. Like Find My, its App Store privacy label has no "Data Used to Track You" section, and the listing doesn't say it contains advertising.

Spending alerts, if you turn them on, go through Plaid. That data is never used for ads or sold.

## Questions about Find My, Life360, and Looop

### Is Life360 or Find My better?

It depends on your family. Find My is free and built into every iPhone, and it's a great fit for simple location sharing when everyone uses Apple devices. Life360 works on iPhone and Android and adds driving reports, crash detection on paid plans, and 24/7 emergency dispatch on Gold and Platinum. Looop works alongside Find My and sends a heads-up when it might be time to call.

### Mom already shares her location in Find My. Do I need Looop?

Maybe not. Find My shows where someone is. Looop tells you how her day is going, with a heads-up about severe weather, nearby crime, or a day that looks unusual. Many families use both, with Find My for where and Looop for when to call.

### Is Apple's Check In enough for checking on a parent?

Check In works well for a single trip or evening. Apple says each session is started by the person being checked on, with a timer or a destination. Looop has nothing for a parent to start. [Read more about Check In for parents](../answers/apple-check-in-for-parents.html).

### Can I use Find My and Looop together?

Yes. Looop works alongside Find My. Keep Find My for sharing locations and finding devices, and add Looop for one calm answer each day.

### Does Looop have SOS or crash detection?

No. Looop has no crash detection, dispatch service, or roadside help, but 911 is one tap away, and you can save up to three emergency contacts for each Loved One. Looop is for knowing when to check in, and it doesn't detect emergencies. If you think someone is in danger, call 911 first.
