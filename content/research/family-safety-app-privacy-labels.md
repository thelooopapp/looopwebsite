# What Family Safety Apps' App Store Privacy Labels Say (2026)

What does a family safety app say it does with your family's data? Apple asks every developer to answer that on the app's App Store page, in a section called App Privacy. On October 26, 2026, we read the privacy labels of 25 family safety apps and put what they say in one table.

This is a review of what the developers themselves declare. We use Apple's terms throughout, and we report every app the same way, including our own app, Looop, which is one of the 25.

## Key numbers

- **14 of 15** family location apps we reviewed declare "Data Used to Track You" on their App Store privacy labels.
- **7 of 15** family location apps list "Contains: Advertising" on their App Store pages.
- **16 of 24** apps from other developers declare "Data Used to Track You." **Identifiers** is the most common data type in that section, on 14 of those 16 labels.
- **0 of 5** apps from Apple, Google, Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile declare "Data Used to Track You," and none lists advertising.
- **7 apps** from other developers have neither: Find My, Google Family Link, Verizon Family, AT&T Secure Family, T-Mobile FamilyMode, Kidslox, and OurPact.

## How we did it

**What Apple's labels are.** Every App Store listing has an App Privacy section, often called a privacy label. The developer fills it in. Each label opens by saying the developer "indicated that the app's privacy practices may include handling of data as described below," and adds, "This information has not been verified by Apple."

A label sorts data into up to three sections:

- **Data Used to Track You:** "The following data may be used to track you across apps and websites owned by other companies."
- **Data Linked to You:** "The following data may be collected and linked to your identity."
- **Data Not Linked to You:** "The following data may be collected but it is not linked to your identity."

**Which apps.** The 24 family safety apps Looop compares itself with, plus Looop. We grouped them by what they're built for:

- **Family location apps (15):** Life360, Findo, Find Phone (Webpeak), OneLocator, Find my Phone (Geo Track Labs), GeoZilla, Glympse, Family Nest, iSharing, Carpin, Findmykids, Be Closer, Famio, Blink, and Familo.
- **Platform and carrier apps (5):** Apple Find My, Google Family Link, Verizon Family, AT&T Secure Family, and T-Mobile FamilyMode.
- **Car safety (1):** OnStar Guardian.
- **Parental controls (3):** Bark, Kidslox, and OurPact.
- **Safety notifications (1):** Looop.

**What we recorded.** For each app: whether its label has a "Data Used to Track You" section and which data types are in it; which data types it lists as "Data Linked to You"; whether its Age Rating section lists "Contains: Advertising"; how it's priced; and the seller name.

**When and where.** The public US App Store listing for each app at apps.apple.com, checked October 26, 2026. We loaded each listing at least twice. Labels and listings can change at any time, so this is a snapshot of that day.

**What we didn't do.** We didn't test the apps, inspect their code, or watch their network traffic. We didn't rate or rank them. This study reports what each listing says, and nothing more.

## What the labels mean

### "Data Used to Track You"

Apple's [privacy definitions](https://apps.apple.com/us/story/id1539235847) say, "Tracking occurs when data collected about you or your device is linked with third-party data for targeted advertising or advertising measurement purposes." They add, "Tracking can also occur when data about you or your device is shared with a data broker."

Apple also lists cases that don't count as tracking, such as data linked only on your own device and never sent off it in a way that identifies you. For a short explainer, see [what "Data Used to Track You" means on the App Store](../answers/data-used-to-track-you.html).

### "Data Linked to You" and "Data Not Linked to You"

Apple defines linked data as "Data that is linked to your identity (via your account, device, or other details)." Data not linked to you is the opposite. A label also says what each kind of data is used for, using Apple's list of purposes: App Functionality, Analytics, Product Personalization, Developer's Advertising or Marketing, Third-Party Advertising, and Other Purposes.

For a family location app, location linked to you is expected. It's how the app shows family members where each other are. The purpose listed next to it says more than the category alone.

### "Contains: Advertising"

This line appears in the Age Rating section of a listing, separate from the privacy label. The table reports whether it was there on the day we checked.

### The data types, in Apple's words

- **Identifiers:** a User ID ("Such as screen name, handle, account ID, assigned user ID, customer number") or a Device ID ("Such as the device's advertising identifier or other device-level ID").
- **Usage Data:** Product Interaction ("Such as app launches, taps, clicks, scrolling information") and Advertising Data ("Such as information about the advertisements you have viewed").
- **Location:** Precise Location (the same or greater resolution as a latitude and longitude) or Coarse Location (lower resolution).
- **Purchases:** "Your purchases or purchase tendencies."
- **Contact Info:** name, email address, phone number, physical address, or other ways to reach you outside the app.
- **Financial Info:** payment info, or other financial info "Such as salary, income, assets, debts."
- **Health & Fitness:** health and medical data, and "Fitness and exercise data."
- **Diagnostics:** crash logs and performance data, such as launch time and energy use.
- **Other Data:** "Any other data types not mentioned."

- Identifiers: 14 apps

- Location: 5 apps (Find Phone by Webpeak, OneLocator, iSharing, Be Closer, and Famio)

## Notes and limits

- **Labels are self-reported.** Every label is the developer's own account, and each one says it "has not been verified by Apple." We report what the labels say, not what the apps do.
- **A declaration isn't proof of selling data.** A "Data Used to Track You" section means the developer says that data may be used for tracking in Apple's sense: linked with other companies' data for ads or ad measurement, or shared with a data broker. The label doesn't say which. This study makes no claim that any app sells data.
- **No section isn't a guarantee either.** An app without the section is, by its own label, not using data for tracking. That's still the developer's statement.
- **Categories aren't amounts.** Two apps can list the same data type and handle very different amounts of it. Labels list types, not volumes.
- **Labels change.** Developers can update a label at any time. This is what each listing showed on October 26, 2026.
- **Price models.** All 25 apps are free to download. Six of the 15 family location apps list in-app purchases named as weekly plans.

### Apps with clean labels

Seven apps from other developers have no "Data Used to Track You" section and no "Contains: Advertising" line:

- **Apple Find My and Google Family Link,** the two platform apps.
- **Verizon Family, AT&T Secure Family, and T-Mobile FamilyMode,** the three carrier apps.
- **Kidslox,** whose listing also says, "Kidslox contains no advertisements."
- **OurPact,** the leanest label in the group: one data type, Contact Info, listed for App Functionality.

Family Nest is the one family location app without a "Data Used to Track You" section. Its listing does show "Contains: Advertising," and its [privacy policy](https://www.familynestapp.com/privacy-policy) says, "We do not sell personal data to third parties."
