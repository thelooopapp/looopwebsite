## What Life360 does well

**Subtitle:** Life360 is one of the best-known family apps, and it does a lot for families with kids and new drivers.

- **iPhone and Android.** Life360 works on iPhone and Android, so a whole family can join, whatever phones they use.
- **Emergency dispatch on paid plans.** Gold and Platinum plans include SOS with 24/7 emergency dispatch and 24/7 roadside assistance.
- **Crash detection.** Paid plans include crash detection, which matters to a household with a new driver.
- **Place alerts.** Families get alerts for arrivals and departures at the places they save, like home and school, plus alerts when someone doesn't show up.
- **Severe weather alerts.** Life360 offers free severe weather alerts, through AccuWeather.
- **A free plan.** Life360 has a free plan, with Silver, Gold, and Platinum plans for families who want more.

## Where Looop and Life360 part ways

**Question:** "So what's actually different?"
**Subtitle:** Nine differences, with sources.

### Made for families from day one

Looop was designed from the first screen around one question: "Is Mom okay today?" Every alert exists to answer it.

Life360 is adding aging parents to an app built for a different job. Its [aging-parent page](https://www.life360.com/en-ca/plans-pricing-ap) pitches its premium Gold and Platinum plans. On its [Q2 2026 earnings call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/17/life360-lif-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/), CEO Lauren Antonoff named aging parents as a next line of business, alongside advertising and pets. Its head of design [credits his redesign](https://www.jaytrainerdesign.com/project/life360) with a 22% lift in premium conversion. **Looop is an independent startup that answers to the families who use it.**

### Your parent's data is never for sale

Looop never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers. There are no ads and no sponsored offers in your notifications.

Life360's ad business is growing fast. It reported [$22 million in ad revenue in Q2 2026](https://investors.life360.com/static-files/e6bd1845-9bc7-429b-aada-fd260b7fc517), up 315% from a year earlier. Members who land at an airport can get an [Uber ride offer](https://www.life360.com/en-us/press/life360-unveils-advertising-platform-to-connect-brands-with-its-engaged-community-of-40-million-u-s-monthly-active-users) in their landing notification, and its [Place Ads](https://www.life360.com/press/life360-enhances-ad-platform-launch-place-ads-and-uplift) reach families based on the places they visit. Life360 also has a history of selling location data to a dozen data brokers.

### No extra watch app needed

If your Loved One wears an Apple Watch and adds Health & Fitness sharing, Looop reads that data through Apple Health on their iPhone. Nothing new to install on the watch.

Life360 solves a different problem. It released a separate [Life360 for Watch](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6748967349) app in June 2026 for people who wear a watch without carrying a phone, and described it as in beta on its Q2 2026 call.

### A heads-up about an unusually large charge

If you link a bank account, Looop can let you know about unusually large spending, with a threshold you set. A sudden large payment can be an early sign of a scam, so you can call Mom that same day. Bank linking is optional, runs through Plaid, and is never used for ads or sold.

Life360's free [Subscription Tracker](https://www.life360.com/blog/subscription-tracker), launched in May 2026, does a different job: it pulls in recurring charges across your Circle and alerts you to price changes, new subscriptions, and renewals. Its [premium plans](https://www.life360.com/en-ca/plans-pricing-ap) include identity theft protection and stolen-funds reimbursement, which help after a loss. At a [Goldman Sachs conference](https://in.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/life360-at-goldman-sachs-conference-growth-broadens-beyond-family-tracking-93CH-5589446) in September 2026, it said it is working on scam protection for aging parents.

### Your bank data stays out of ads

Both apps can link a bank account through Plaid. In Looop, it's optional, used only for your family's spending alerts, and never used for ads or sold. Your family's data isn't for sale.

Life360's Subscription Tracker connects checking accounts and credit cards through Plaid, for members 18 and older in the US. Its [setup page](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/39739802477591-Set-Up-Subscription-Tracker) says that by connecting, you agree that "Life360 may use your transaction data to show you relevant ads and measure ad performance in the Life360 app and elsewhere." [What does Life360's Subscription Tracker do with bank data?](looop-vs-life360.html#faq)

### Weather alerts with no ads attached

When severe weather heads toward a Loved One, Looop tells you, in more than 50 countries, including the US. The alert is about the storm and nothing else. It might be a good time to call.

Life360 offers free severe weather alerts too, through AccuWeather. In the [same announcement](https://investors.life360.com/news-releases/news-release-details/life360-partners-accuweather-deliver-real-time-severe-weather), it said it will use AccuWeather data to target ads during weather events, such as a retailer promoting emergency supplies or repair services to members in affected areas.

### Nearby crime, at a distance you choose

Looop sends a calm heads-up when crime is reported near a Loved One, so you can reach out if it feels right. Your family picks the distance: 1, 3, 5, or 10 miles. Crime alerts work in all 50 US states, and there's no daily limit on how many you see.

Life360 shows crime as a layer on its map. Members can turn its [Safety Incidents](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/38226266501911-Safety-Incidents) on or off in Map details. When they're on, recent, high-severity events, like fires, thefts, and weapon-related crimes, appear as pins, and Life360 sends a notification when one meets its criteria. They cover the last six hours, within one mile of you or a Circle member, in the US only. Free Circles can see details for up to three incidents in 24 hours; Gold and Platinum see more. Its older [Crime Reports](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053642245399-Life360-Crime-Reports) feature, on paid plans, still has its own help page.

In Looop, crime is one part of a holistic approach to family care: weather, crime, spending, health, fitness, activity, and the shape of each day. Each day, all of it comes together in one answer: a Typical Day, or an Unusual Day that might be worth a call. [What do Life360's Safety Incidents show?](looop-vs-life360.html#faq)

### Looop sticks to one job

Looop is built to tell you when to check in, and it uses only what it needs to tell whether something unusual is going on.

Life360 is aiming wider. In a [May 2026 post](https://chrishulls.medium.com/life360-is-building-a-family-ai-team-come-build-it-with-us-f6a3cd19ef20), Executive Chairman Chris Hulls describes a family super app with calendar events, bills, RSVPs, and a drawer for documents like insurance cards. He calls the approach "aggressive on permissions, anchored in trust." Some families will love that.

### Made only for people

Looop is built around people and their routines. Mom keeps her day, and you get a heads-up when it's worth a call.

Life360 brings people, pets, and things into one app. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/life360-family-safety-gps/id384830320) includes Tile trackers for items and a Pet GPS, and in [August 2026](https://investors.life360.com/news-releases/news-release-details/life360-announces-new-pet-care-products-so-no-one-forgets-fido) it added new pet products, including a Pet Tag and activity alerts for dogs. That's handy for a busy household.

## Questions families ask

**Is Looop or Life360 better for my family?**
Looop is built for families who care from a distance after the driving years: kids at college or in a big city, parents living separately or a few states away, a relative living alone, or siblings and friends who look out for each other. It tells you when to check in, so the people you love can live their lives. Choose Life360 if you want to pay more for precision GPS tracking, driving details recorded and stored, call center support, targeted ads, and your family's data being shared with its partners. Choose Looop if you want safety alerts instead of tracking, a nudge when a drive looks unusual, no ads ever, and your family's data never for sale.

**Can I switch from Life360 to Looop?**
Yes. Download Looop, sign up with your phone number, and invite your Loved Ones from your contacts. They join with a simple six-character code. Before you send invites, talk it through together and show everyone what Looop shares. If you pay for a Life360 plan, remember to cancel it the way you signed up.

**Does Looop sell my data?**
No. Looop never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers. There are no ads in the app and no sponsored offers in your notifications. You can delete your account and data whenever you choose.

**Does Looop work on Android?**
Not today. Looop is available for iPhone on the App Store. If your family includes Android phones, Life360 may be a better fit for now.

**Does Looop have crash detection or emergency dispatch?**
No. Looop doesn't have crash detection, a 24/7 dispatch service, or roadside help. It lets you call 911 in one tap and save up to three emergency contacts for each Loved One. Looop helps you know when to check in. It doesn't detect emergencies. If you think someone is in danger, call 911 first.

**Does Looop have a map?**
Yes, as a supporting feature. After a nudge, the family map is there to help you decide what to do next. Looop is built around notifications, so you don't need to keep checking it.

**Will my mom know she's in our Looop?**
Yes. Every Loved One joins by invitation. Health & Fitness sharing, like heart rate, sleep, steps, and workouts, is available and optional.

**Does Looop use my bank data for ads?**
No. Bank linking in Looop is optional and runs through Plaid. It's used only for spending alerts, like a heads-up about an unusually large charge, and it's never used for ads or sold. Life360's Subscription Tracker also connects checking accounts and credit cards through Plaid. Its [setup page](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/39739802477591-Set-Up-Subscription-Tracker) says that by connecting, you agree that "Life360 may use your transaction data to show you relevant ads and measure ad performance in the Life360 app and elsewhere."

**What does Life360's Subscription Tracker do with bank data?**
Subscription Tracker connects checking accounts and credit cards through Plaid and finds recurring charges, with alerts for price changes, new subscriptions, and renewals. Life360's [setup page](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/39739802477591-Set-Up-Subscription-Tracker) says that by connecting, you agree that "Life360 may use your transaction data to show you relevant ads and measure ad performance in the Life360 app and elsewhere."

**Does Life360 have crime alerts?**
Yes, as a layer on its map. Members can turn Life360's [Safety Incidents](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/38226266501911-Safety-Incidents) on or off in Map details. They show recent, high-severity events, like fires, thefts, and weapon-related crimes, as pins, with a notification. They cover the last six hours, within one mile of you or a Circle member, in the US. Free Circles can see details for up to three incidents in 24 hours. Looop's crime alerts work in all 50 US states at the distance your family chooses: 1, 3, 5, or 10 miles.

**Where do crime alerts work?**
Crime alerts work in all 50 US states, at the distance you choose: 1, 3, 5, or 10 miles. Severe weather alerts work in more than 50 countries, including the US, Canada, Mexico, most of Europe, Israel, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, India, China, the Philippines, Brazil, South Africa, the UAE, and Singapore.
