## "Do I really need to see every turn?"

### The driving years were one chapter

**Subtitle:** This is the next one.

When the kids got their licenses, you wanted to see every drive. That made sense. Now they're in college or out on their own. Maybe your parents live separately or a few states away. Maybe your aunt lives alone, and nobody stops by every day.

You don't need to follow their every move. You want them to have their independence, and you want to know when their day looks unusual.

## Sound familiar?

**Subtitle:** Looop is for the life events that come after the learner's permit.

- **"She's off to college." Trust them more.** You don't need a report on every drive anymore. You'd just like to know if a day looks off, and otherwise let her live it.
- **"He moved to the big city." Safe from afar.** His new neighborhood is busier than the one he grew up in. Get a heads-up when crime is reported near him, in all 50 US states.
- **"Help isn't down the street anymore." Parents on their own.** Know when Dad's been home longer than usual for him, or when he arrives at a hospital or ER.
- **"Aunt Ira lives alone." Someone living solo.** Nobody stops by every day. Get a nudge if her phone hasn't been picked up in hours, or if her day looks different.
- **"Mom moved to the coast." A different forecast.** A storm on her coast won't show up in your forecast. Looop sends severe weather alerts in more than 50 countries.
- **"Everyone counts on me." The one in the middle.** Kids in two cities, parents in two homes. One calm screen, and one heads-up when someone needs a call.

## "Is Mom okay today?"

### More than where they are. How their day is going.

Most family apps are built around a map: they answer "Where is she?" Looop answers "Is Mom okay today?" It takes a holistic approach to family care: weather, crime, spending, health, fitness, activity, and the shape of each day.

- **What's around them.** Severe weather heading their way, in more than 50 countries, and crime reported nearby, in all 50 US states.
- **Arrivals that matter.** Know when they arrive at a hospital or ER, a police station, or an airport.
- **How long they've been there.** A nudge when they've been home much longer than is normal for them.
- **Unusual Day.** A gentle nudge when driving, walking, or phone use doesn't fit their own normal routine, or their phone hasn't been picked up in hours.
- **Spending, if they link an account.** An optional heads-up about an unusually large charge, with a threshold you set. That can be an early sign of a scam.

## "Is Dad still getting his walks in?"

### How they're feeling. How they're moving.

**Subtitle:** Health & Fitness sharing is available if you need it.

A restless night. A heart rate that's high for them. A week without the usual walks. With Health & Fitness sharing, Looop gives you a heads-up when something is off for them.

- **Health.** Heart rate, breathing rate, and sleep. Get a heads-up about an unusually high or low heart rate or an unusual breathing rate.
- **Fitness.** Steps, walking, and workouts, from a morning run to a session at the gym. See whether Mom is as active as usual for her.

Based on your family's situation, you may want to add Health & Fitness sharing. It's optional, and works with Apple Health and Apple Watch.

Each day, all of it comes together in one answer: a Typical Day, or an Unusual Day that might be worth a call.

## "I'm an adult. I don't need to be watched."

### A word for the grown-ups you love

**Subtitle:** You're right. Looop doesn't watch you.

Looop sends your family alerts, and only when something seems off. You live your day. They get fewer "where are you?" texts, and you get fewer "just checking on you" calls.

- **You decide whether to join.** Looop works by invitation, so nobody signs you up without your knowing.
- **Health & Fitness sharing and spending alerts are available.** Add them if they fit your family.
- **Your family's data isn't for sale.** No advertisers. Ever.
- **You can change your mind.** Leave a group anytime.

Looop helps you know when to check in. It doesn't detect emergencies. If you think someone is in danger, call 911.
