# Looop Adds Spending Alerts That Can Flag Possible Scams Early

## An optional heads-up about unusually large spending on a linked account can be an early sign that something is wrong, so families can call the same day

**[CITY, STATE] - [LAUNCH DATE]** - Looop, the safety notifications app for families who care from a distance, today announced spending alerts. When a Loved One chooses to link a bank account, Looop can send the family a heads-up about unusually large spending, based on a threshold the family sets. A sudden large payment can be an early sign of a scam, and the alert gives families a reason to call that day and simply ask.

Scams that reach older adults often move fast. The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center reports that people 60 and older filed 201,266 complaints in 2025 and reported $7.748 billion in losses, up 59% from 2024 ([2025 IC3 Annual Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)). The Federal Trade Commission says older adults report the highest total losses to fraud paid by bank transfer, and that scammers often press people to move money to "protect it" ([Protecting Older Consumers, 2024-2025](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P144400-OlderAdultsReportDec2025.pdf)). A family that hears about an unusual payment early has a better chance to talk before more money moves.

"[DRAFT QUOTE - FOUNDER TO APPROVE] Scams are designed to make people act alone and fast. No app can stop a scammer from calling. But a heads-up about an unusual payment gives a family a reason to call Mom that afternoon and ask, 'Hey, was that you?' Most of the time the answer is yes, and that's fine. When it isn't, the family is talking sooner," said [FOUNDER NAME], founder of Looop.

**How spending alerts work**

- **Optional bank linking.** Bank linking is optional and runs through Plaid. A Loved One decides whether to link an account.
- **A threshold the family sets.** Families choose what counts as unusually large, so small, everyday purchases stay quiet.
- **A heads-up, not a verdict.** A large payment is often perfectly normal. The alert simply suggests it might be a good time to check in.
- **Alerts on your terms.** Families choose exactly which alerts they get about each Loved One and can turn any alert off with one tap.
- **One-tap check-in.** Call, text or FaceTime straight from a Loved One's card.

Looop does not detect or prevent scams, and it does not reimburse losses. If a family suspects a scam, they should contact the bank right away and report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and to the FBI at ic3.gov.

**A heads-up before, not only help after**

Some family safety plans focus on what happens after money is gone. Life360, for example, lists identity theft protection and stolen funds reimbursement of up to $25,000 on its Gold plan and up to $1 million on its Platinum plan ([Life360 plans](https://www.life360.com/en-ca/plans-pricing-ap)), and said in September 2026 that it is working on scam protection for aging parents ([Goldman Sachs conference coverage](https://in.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/life360-at-goldman-sachs-conference-growth-broadens-beyond-family-tracking-93CH-5589446)). Looop's approach is different: a calm nudge to talk while there may still be time to act.

**Private by design**

Spending data is sensitive. Looop never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers. Every Loved One joins by invitation and chooses what to share.
