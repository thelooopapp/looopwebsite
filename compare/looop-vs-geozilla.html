Looop vs.
GeoZilla

GeoZilla puts your family on a map.

It adds driving reports and an Apple Watch app.

Looop tells you when Mom's day looks off.

A family locator and a daily heads-up

GeoZilla is a family locator built around a shared map, with place alerts, location history, a help alert, driving alerts, and an Apple Watch app. Looop is a safety notifications app for families caring from a distance, and it sums up each Loved One's day as typical or worth a call.

What GeoZilla does well

It covers the core family locator features, on your wrist too.

Place alerts and history

Families get alerts when someone arrives at or leaves a place, and can look back at where a circle member has been that day.

A help alert

A member can send an emergency alert that lets the family know they need help.

Driving alerts

Premium alerts flag speeding and phone use while driving, which many families of new drivers want.

An Apple Watch app

GeoZilla runs on Apple Watch and Apple Vision as well as iPhone.

Consent built in

Its listing says location sharing requires explicit consent from every circle member.

Check-ins and chat

Members can leave check-ins and comments for each other in the app.

"How is Looop different from GeoZilla?"

Where Looop goes its own way

Looop is built for checking on a parent who lives far away.

  • One answer about everyone's day

    Looop takes a holistic approach to family care: weather, crime, spending, health, fitness, activity, and the shape of each day. It's all in one place, so you're not checking five apps to piece together how everyone's doing.

    Each day, all of it comes together in one answer: a Typical Day, or an Unusual Day that might be worth a call.

  • The map comes second

    Looop is built to answer "Is Mom okay today?" It stays quiet when all is well. The family map is there for after a nudge, when you need it.

  • For a parent who wants her independence

    GeoZilla's description says it suits an elderly loved one or a teenager. Looop is built around adult children caring from a distance, and around a parent who wants to keep her independence. She joins by invitation, and nothing changes about her day.

  • What the App Store labels show

    Looop never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers. Looop's App Store privacy label has no "Data Used to Track You" section. GeoZilla's label lists identifiers under "Data Used to Track You."

Looop isn't an emergency service. It doesn't call 911 for you, send help, or detect emergencies. If you think someone is in danger, call 911 first.

"Is Looop right for us?"

Where GeoZilla may fit better

GeoZilla may be the better pick in these cases.

A teen driver

If you want alerts every time a new driver speeds or picks up the phone, GeoZilla's driving alerts go further than Looop's nudge when a drive looks unusual.

A watch-first family

GeoZilla has its own Apple Watch app. Looop reads Apple Watch health data through Apple Health on the iPhone.

A help button

If you want a one-tap help alert for the person being looked after, GeoZilla has one. Looop doesn't.

Plan choices

GeoZilla sells weekly, monthly, quarterly, and yearly plans. Looop is free for the first two weeks.

Questions families ask

Is Looop like GeoZilla?

Not in how it works. GeoZilla is a family locator built around a map, with place alerts, history, and driving alerts. Looop is a safety notifications app that brings weather, crime, spending, health, fitness, activity, and the shape of each day together, and stays quiet when all is well.

Can I use GeoZilla and Looop together?

Yes. Looop works on its own. Some families keep a map app for a teen driver and use Looop for a parent who lives a few states away.

Does Looop work for aging parents?

Yes. That's who it's built for. Looop tells you when Mom's day looks unusual, when a storm heads her way, or when crime is reported nearby, so you know when to call. She keeps her routine, and you get fewer "just checking" calls to make.

Does Looop have an SOS button or crash detection?

No. Looop isn't an emergency service. It doesn't call 911 for you, send help, or detect emergencies. If you think someone is in danger, call 911 first. You can call 911 or a Loved One's emergency contacts in one tap from the app.

Does Looop sell my data?

No. Looop never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers. There are no ads in the app.
