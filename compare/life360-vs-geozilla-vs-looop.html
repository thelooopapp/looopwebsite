"Where does each one shine?"

What each app does well, and where it may fit better

Life360

What Life360 does well

Life360 is the family map many people already know. It's built around a live family map with location history, place alerts, and driving reports, and it works on iPhone and Android. Paid plans add crash detection, and Gold and Platinum include SOS with 24/7 emergency dispatch and roadside help.

Life360 has added more than location. Severe weather alerts come through AccuWeather. Its Safety Incidents feature, new in 2026, puts recent, high-severity events within one mile on the map, with a notification. Its free Subscription Tracker links bank accounts through Plaid to show recurring charges across a Circle.

On data, Life360 runs a growing ad business. Its setup page says that by connecting an account to Subscription Tracker, you agree that "Life360 may use your transaction data to show you relevant ads and measure ad performance in the Life360 app and elsewhere." Life360 has a history of selling location data to a dozen data brokers.

Where Life360 may fit better: a household with a new teen driver, families with Android phones, or anyone who wants crash detection on paid plans and 24/7 emergency dispatch on Gold and Platinum.

Choose Life360 if you want to pay more for precision GPS tracking, driving details recorded and stored, call center support, targeted ads, and your family's data being shared with its partners.

GeoZilla

What GeoZilla does well

GeoZilla, listed on the App Store by GZ TECH LTD, is a family map. You get alerts when family members arrive at or leave a place, daily location history, check-ins, chat, battery levels, and an Emergency Alert that lets family know someone needs help.

For drivers, it shows whether a family member is speeding or using their phone while driving, and Premium adds Driver Protection. It has an Apple Watch app. Its description says it suits families with a teenager or an older loved one.

Plans are flexible: weekly from $5.99 to $11.99, monthly at $19.99, quarterly at $39.99, and yearly at $49.99 or $69.99, according to its App Store listing. Its privacy label lists identifiers under "Data Used to Track You," including a user ID used for third-party advertising.

Where GeoZilla may fit better: families with kids or teens who want a shared map, place alerts, driving insights, and an Apple Watch app, and who like the option of a weekly plan.

Looop

Best for grown-up families

We make Looop, so weigh this part with that in mind. It's a safety notifications app from LooopApp LLC, an independent Austin startup that launched in May 2026.

Looop's approach is holistic family care: weather, crime, spending, health, fitness, activity, and the shape of each day. You get a heads-up when severe weather heads toward a Loved One, in more than 50 countries, and when crime is reported nearby, in all 50 US states, at 1, 3, 5, or 10 miles. If driving, walking, or phone activity looks unusual, you get a gentle nudge. Spending alerts and Health & Fitness sharing through Apple Health are available if you need them.

Each day, all of it comes together in one answer: a Typical Day, or an Unusual Day that might be worth a call. A family map is there too, for when a nudge makes you want to look.

Looop has no ads, ever, and your family's data is never for sale. It's iPhone only, and it isn't an emergency service. It doesn't send help or detect crashes, though 911 and up to three emergency contacts for each Loved One are one tap away.

Choose Looop if you want safety alerts instead of tracking, a nudge when a drive looks unusual, no ads ever, and your family's data never for sale. Peace of mind for you, independence for them.

"How is Looop built differently?"

What Looop tells you

Looop answers one question each day: "Is Mom okay today?"

  • No more piecing the day together

    It's all in one place, so you're not checking five apps to piece together how everyone's doing.

    Looop brings weather, crime, spending, health, fitness, activity, and the shape of each day together in one answer. It's free for the first two weeks, with no weekly plans.

  • You hear from Looop when it matters

    On a normal day, Looop stays quiet. When something looks off, you get a heads-up, so there's no screen to keep checking and no "where are you?" text every evening.

  • Made for grown-up families

    Looop is made for life after the driving years: a parent a few states away, kids at college, a sibling or friend who lives alone. Each Loved One joins by invitation and keeps their own routine.

  • Looop doesn't work with advertisers

    Looop never sells your data. Its App Store privacy label has no "Data Used to Track You" section, and the listing doesn't say it contains advertising.

    If you link an account for spending alerts, it goes through Plaid, and that data is never used for ads or sold.

Questions about Life360, GeoZilla, and Looop

Is Life360 or GeoZilla better?

Life360 has more emergency features, like crash detection on paid plans and 24/7 emergency dispatch on Gold and Platinum, plus severe weather alerts and Safety Incidents. GeoZilla offers a family map, driving insights, an Emergency Alert, and an Apple Watch app, with weekly, monthly, or yearly plans. Looop is worth a look if the person you worry about is a grown-up who lives far away.

Does GeoZilla work for aging parents?

GeoZilla's description says it suits families with a teenager or an older loved one, and it offers place alerts and an Emergency Alert. Looop is built for checking on a parent from a distance, with weather, crime, and unusual activity in one daily answer.

Can I use Looop alongside Life360 or GeoZilla?

Yes. If your family likes having a map, keep it. Some families keep a map for the kids and use Looop for the people they care for from a distance.

Does Looop have SOS or crash detection?

No. Looop has no crash detection, dispatch service, or roadside help. You can call 911 in one tap and save up to three emergency contacts for each Loved One. Looop tells you when it might be time to check in, but it doesn't detect emergencies. If you think someone is in danger, call 911 first.

Does Looop show ads or sell my data?

No. Looop has no ads and no sponsored offers in your notifications. It never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers.
