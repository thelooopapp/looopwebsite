The Best Life360 Alternatives in 2026, Including iSharing, GeoZilla, and Family Nest

By the Looop Team·October 16, 2026·Updated October 26, 2026·11 min read

"Is there a way to know they're okay without watching their every move?"

If you're searching for a Life360 alternative, there's a good chance that question brought you here. Your kids may be grown, and the family map started to feel like too much. A parent may have said, kindly but firmly, "I don't need to be followed around." Some people land here after reading how some family apps make money.

We compared nine options with Life360. Three of them, iSharing, GeoZilla, and Family Nest, work a lot like it. The rest are the tools already on your phone, an emergency service for drivers, and our own app, Looop. Each one is good at something. We'll tell you who each fits, including when the answer isn't Looop.

Want the whole field in one table? See Family safety apps compared.

Facts about each company come from its own help pages, privacy policies, App Store listings, and filings, or from reputable reporting, linked throughout. iSharing, GeoZilla, Family Nest, OnStar Guardian, and Life360's Safety Incidents and Subscription Tracker were checked October 26, 2026; the rest in October 2026. Prices are from US App Store listings and change often.

How we compared

We looked at the same four things for every app:

  • Notifications. What does it tell you about, and when?
  • Privacy and ads. Does it show ads, or sell or share data, according to its own policy or reputable reporting?
  • Independence. Does it give the people you love room to live their day, and do they choose what's shared?
  • Platforms. Does it work on iPhone, Android, or both?

Life360: the baseline

What it is. Life360 is one of the best-known family apps. It's a GPS location tracking app built around a live family map and location history. Its location sharing page highlights Place Alerts for arrivals and departures, and alerts when someone doesn't show up where they're expected.

Notifications. Life360 also sends severe weather alerts through AccuWeather, and it shows crime. Its Safety Incidents put recent, high-severity events, like fires, thefts, and weapon-related crimes, on the map as pins, with a notification. They cover the last six hours, within one mile of you or a Circle member, in the US. Free Circles can see details for up to three incidents in 24 hours.

Who it fits. Families with younger kids who want a shared map and detailed arrival and departure alerts, on both iPhone and Android.

Privacy and ads. This is where many people start looking elsewhere. Life360 launched an advertising platform in 2024 with Uber as a key partner, tied into notifications when members land at an airport. In June 2025 it launched Place Ads, which reach families based on real-world places they visit.

Its free Subscription Tracker, added in May 2026, links checking accounts and credit cards through Plaid to find recurring charges. Its setup page says that by connecting, you agree that "Life360 may use your transaction data to show you relevant ads and measure ad performance."

Life360 also has a history of selling location data to a dozen data brokers. The Markup reported in 2021 that its buyers included Cuebiq, X-Mode, SafeGraph, and Allstate's Arity. Life360 then stopped most of those sales, kept Arity, and began selling aggregated data to Placer.ai. In a 2024 earnings release, it said its data business had returned to growth.

Platforms. iPhone and Android, with app links on Life360's site.

iSharing

What it is. iSharing, from iSharingSoft in Irvine, California, is one of the apps closest to Life360. Its App Store listing describes a live map with Street View, place alerts for home, work, and school, location history from 7 days to a year depending on plan, a panic alert that records sound, messaging, and Meetups for temporary group sharing.

Notifications. Its Elderly Care feature sends an alert when an older person doesn't use their phone. Battery and inactivity alerts come with Silver and up, and driving reports for speeding, sudden braking, and phone use come with Gold and up.

Privacy and ads. iSharing's privacy policy, last revised May 21, 2026, says: "We may also share location information with third parties for advertising, research, and analytic purposes." Its App Store privacy label declares "Data Used to Track You," including location, identifiers, and usage data.

Price. Free, with premium plans from $7.99 a month or $69.99 a year on the App Store, and a seven-day free trial.

Who it fits. Families who want a Life360-style map with SOS and driving reports, plus an alert if an older parent's phone goes quiet.

GeoZilla

What it is. GeoZilla, from GZ TECH LTD, calls itself a family GPS tracker. Its App Store listing describes place arrival alerts, location history, check-ins, chat, battery levels, a help alert, driving insights for speeding and phone use, and an Apple Watch app. Its description says it suits families with older relatives as well as teens.

Privacy and ads. Its App Store privacy label declares "Data Used to Track You" for identifiers.

Price. Plans on the App Store run from weekly ($5.99 to $11.99) to yearly (up to $69.99). Several are weekly, so check the billing period before you subscribe.

Who it fits. Families, especially with teens, who want a map with check-ins, driving insights, and an Apple Watch app.

See Looop vs. GeoZilla and Life360 vs. GeoZilla vs. Looop.

Family Nest (formerly Family360)

What it is. If you've seen "Life360 vs. Family360," this is that app. Its store pages now read "Family Nest (ex Family360)." Families join private "Nests" with chat, a live map, place alerts for home, school, and work, over-speed alerts, a phone locator, and SOS, all free, according to its App Store listing. Premium adds real-time tracking with ETA, 365 days of history, and travel statistics. It also has a weekly Nest Drive driving score and shows the weather through OpenWeatherMap.

Privacy and ads. Family Nest's privacy policy, effective September 23, 2026, says: "We do not sell personal data to third parties." Its App Store listing notes that the app contains advertising.

Price. $5.99 a month or $39.99 a year on the App Store.

Platforms. iPhone and Android.

Who it fits. Families with kids, new drivers, couples, or a travel group who want an affordable shared map with SOS and speed alerts, across iPhone and Android.

See Looop vs. Family Nest, Life360 vs. Family Nest vs. Looop, and Is Family Nest the same as Family360?

Apple Find My

What it is. If everyone in your family uses an iPhone, you already have Find My. You can share your location with family and friends for an hour, until the end of the day, indefinitely, or for a custom time.

Notifications. Find My can notify you when someone arrives at a place, leaves it, or isn't there during a set schedule. Apple handles consent well here. Your friend gets an alert when you set a notification, and they must approve recurring ones.

Apple's Messages app also has Check In, which tells a trusted contact when you've arrived. Apple says Check In shares your location only if there's an unexpected delay and you don't respond.

Privacy. Apple's Find My privacy page says that when you and your friend are both on iOS 17 or later, your location isn't accessible to Apple. It says shared location is kept for up to 24 hours to provide the service, then deleted.

Platforms. Apple devices. Sharing your ongoing Live Location requires an iPhone on both ends, according to Apple.

Who it fits. All-iPhone families who want simple, free location sharing and a light check-in for trips. Looop works alongside Find My.

See Find My and Looop and Life360 vs. Find My vs. Looop.

Google Maps location sharing and Find Hub

What it is. Google lets you share your real-time location through several of its apps, including Maps and Find Hub. The people you share with can see your name, photo, real-time location, and battery status, and sometimes where you've recently been.

Notifications. In Google Maps, you can add notifications for when someone who shares with you arrives at or leaves a place, up to five per person. The person gets an email letting them know, and they can turn these notifications off. Find Hub offers the same arrive and leave alerts, and lets you share for an hour, until the end of the day, until you stop, or for a custom time.

Privacy and ads. Google says it never sells your personal information to anyone. Its privacy policy also says it collects location information to offer features including ads based on your location, depending on your settings.

Platforms. Google Maps location sharing works on iPhone and Android, so it suits mixed families.

Who it fits. Families with a mix of iPhones and Android phones who want free, straightforward location sharing.

Google Family Link

What it is. Family Link is Google's parental controls app. Location is one part of it, alongside screen time limits, app approvals, and content filters. It's built for kids and teens.

Notifications. Parents can see a child's device on a map and get alerts when the child arrives at or leaves a place, according to Google.

Privacy and ads. Google's Family Link FAQ says kids may see ads in Google services, but not personalized ones. The same FAQ says teens need a parent's approval to stop supervision.

Platforms. Parents can use Family Link on Android or iPhone. Only Android devices, Chromebooks, and some Fitbit devices can be supervised. Google notes that iPhones can't be.

Who it fits. Parents of younger kids with Android phones who want location plus screen time controls. It isn't a fit for adult family members.

Glympse

What it is. Glympse focuses on temporary location sharing. You set a timer, send a link, and your location stops sharing when the time runs out. Viewers don't need the app. Today much of Glympse's business serves companies, for things like delivery and technician arrival updates.

Notifications. Glympse's family use is built around sharing for a set time. Ongoing alerts aren't its focus.

Privacy and ads. Glympse's privacy policy says it may work with a third party to show ads in its apps. Its App Store listing lists usage data used for third-party advertising. The same policy says Glympse has not sold personal information under California's legal definition, though it may share aggregate information.

Platforms. iPhone and Android.

Who it fits. Anyone sharing their location for one drive, trip, or night out, without an always-on connection.

See Looop vs. Glympse and Life360 vs. Glympse vs. Looop.

OnStar Guardian

What it is. OnStar Guardian is emergency and roadside help for drivers, and it works in any vehicle, in the US and Canada. Its App Store listing describes Mobile Crash Response that connects to an OnStar advisor, an Emergency button with 24/7 advisors who can send first responders, roadside help, and location sharing with arrival alerts.

Privacy and ads. Its App Store privacy label declares "Data Used to Track You" for usage data.

Price. $15 a month as a standalone plan, or included for OnStar members. It can be shared with up to seven people.

Who it fits. A parent who still drives, when you want crash response and someone who can send help. It isn't a replacement for a daily heads-up, and Looop isn't a replacement for it. Help in an emergency is one thing. Knowing when to call, before there is one, is another. Many families could use both.

See OnStar Guardian and Looop.

Looop

What it is. Looop is our app, so read this section with that in mind. Looop is a safety notifications app from an independent Austin startup that launched in May 2026. It tells you when it might be worth checking in, so you don't have to keep looking at a map.

Notifications. Looop gives you a heads-up when severe weather is heading toward a Loved One, when crime is reported near them in all 50 US states at the distance you choose (1, 3, 5, or 10 miles), and when their day looks unusual compared to their own routine. When all is well, it stays quiet. You choose which alerts you get for each person.

Looop takes a holistic approach to family care: weather, crime, spending, health, fitness, activity, and the shape of each day. Each day, all of it comes together in one answer: a Typical Day, or an Unusual Day that might be worth a call.

Independence. Loved Ones join by invitation. Health & Fitness sharing, like heart rate, sleep, and steps, is available and optional. So is a heads-up about an unusually large charge, if a Loved One links an account through Plaid. There's a family map for when you need it, for example after a nudge to check in.

Privacy and ads. Looop never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers. Linked bank data is used only for your family's alerts, never for ads.

Emergencies. Looop isn't an emergency service. It doesn't call 911 for you, send help, or detect crashes. You can call 911 or a Loved One's emergency contacts in one tap.

Price. Free for the first two weeks.

Platforms. iPhone only, which is a real limit if anyone in your family uses Android.

Who it fits. Adults who care from a distance, like grown kids with parents in another state or parents of college students, who want to know when to call without hovering.

Choose Life360 if you want to pay more for precision GPS tracking, driving details recorded and stored, call center support, targeted ads, and your family's data being shared with its partners.

Choose Looop if you want safety alerts instead of tracking, a nudge when a drive looks unusual, no ads ever, and your family's data never for sale. Peace of mind for you, independence for them.

Which one is right for you?

Start with the people on the other end. What would they agree to, and what would they be glad to have?

  • Young kids with Android phones: Google Family Link.
  • An all-iPhone family that wants simple sharing: Apple Find My.
  • A mix of iPhone and Android: Google Maps location sharing.
  • A Life360-style map with SOS, driving reports, and an alert when a parent's phone goes quiet: iSharing.
  • A map with check-ins, driving insights, and an Apple Watch app: GeoZilla.
  • An affordable family map on iPhone and Android: Family Nest.
  • Sharing for one trip or night out: Glympse.
  • A parent who still drives and could need help after a crash: OnStar Guardian, alongside a daily heads-up from Looop.
  • You'll pay more for precision GPS tracking and driving details recorded and stored, and you're fine with targeted ads: Life360.
  • Safety alerts instead of tracking, no ads ever, and your family's data never for sale: Looop.

Whatever you choose, talk with each person first, show them what's shared, and make it easy for them to say no.
