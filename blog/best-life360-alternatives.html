The Best Life360 Alternatives in 2026

By the Looop team·October 6, 2026·7 min read

"Is there a way to know they're okay without watching their every move?"

If you're searching for a Life360 alternative, there's a good chance that question brought you here. Maybe your kids are grown and the family map started to feel like too much. Maybe a parent said, kindly but firmly, "I don't need to be followed around." Or maybe you read how some family apps make money and want something more private.

This is an honest roundup. We compared five options against Life360, including our own app, Looop. Each one is good at something. We'll tell you who each fits, including when the answer isn't Looop.

Facts about each company come from its own help pages, privacy policies, app store listings and filings, or from reputable reporting, linked throughout. Checked October 2026.

How we compared

We looked at the same four things for every app:

  • Notifications. What does it tell you about, and when?
  • Privacy and ads. Does it show ads, or sell or share data, according to its own policy or reputable reporting?
  • Independence. Does it give the people you love room to live their day, and do they choose what's shared?
  • Platforms. Does it work on iPhone, Android, or both?

Life360: the baseline

What it is. Life360 is one of the best-known family apps. It's a GPS location tracking app built around a live family map and location history. Its location sharing page highlights Place Alerts for arrivals and departures, and alerts when someone doesn't show up where they're expected.

Who it fits. Families with younger kids who want a shared map and detailed arrival and departure alerts, on both iPhone and Android.

Privacy and ads. This is where many people start looking elsewhere. Life360 launched an advertising platform in 2024 with Uber as a key partner, tied into notifications when members land at an airport. In June 2025 it launched Place Ads, which reach families based on real-world places they visit.

Life360 also has a history of selling location data to a dozen data brokers. The Markup reported in 2021 that its buyers included Cuebiq, X-Mode, SafeGraph and Allstate's Arity. Life360 then stopped most of those sales, kept Arity, and began selling aggregated data to Placer.ai. In a 2024 earnings release, it said its data business had returned to growth. In short, Life360 still sells location data today.

Platforms. iPhone and Android, with app links on Life360's site.

Best for: families who want the most detailed shared map and are comfortable with an ad-supported business model.

Apple Find My

What it is. If everyone in your family uses an iPhone, you already have Find My. You can share your location with family and friends for an hour, until the end of the day, indefinitely, or for a custom time.

Notifications. Find My can notify you when someone arrives at a place, leaves it, or isn't there during a set schedule. Apple handles consent well here. Your friend gets an alert when you set a notification, and they must approve recurring ones.

Apple's Messages app also has Check In, which tells a trusted contact when you've arrived. Apple says Check In shares your location only if there's an unexpected delay and you don't respond.

Privacy. Apple's Find My privacy page says that when you and your friend are both on iOS 17 or later, your location isn't accessible to Apple. It says shared location is kept for up to 24 hours to provide the service, then deleted.

Platforms. Apple devices. Sharing your ongoing Live Location requires an iPhone on both ends, according to Apple.

Best for: all-iPhone families who want simple location sharing and a light check-in for trips.

Google Maps location sharing and Find Hub

What it is. Google lets you share your real-time location through several of its apps, including Maps and Find Hub. The people you share with can see your name, photo, real-time location and battery status, and sometimes where you've recently been.

Notifications. In Google Maps, you can add notifications for when someone who shares with you arrives at or leaves a place, up to five per person. The person gets an email letting them know, and they can turn these notifications off. Find Hub offers the same arrive and leave alerts, and lets you share for an hour, until the end of the day, until you stop, or for a custom time.

Privacy and ads. Google says it never sells your personal information to anyone. Its privacy policy also says it collects location information to offer features including ads based on your location, depending on your settings.

Platforms. Google Maps location sharing works on iPhone and Android, so it suits mixed families.

Best for: families with a mix of iPhones and Android phones who want free, straightforward location sharing.

Google Family Link

What it is. Family Link is Google's parental controls app. Location is one part of it, alongside screen time limits, app approvals and content filters. It's built for kids and teens, not adults.

Notifications. Parents can see a child's device on a map and get alerts when the child arrives at or leaves a place, according to Google.

Privacy and ads. Google's Family Link FAQ says kids may see ads in Google services, but not personalized ones. The same FAQ says teens need a parent's approval to stop supervision.

Platforms. Parents can use Family Link on Android or iPhone. Only Android devices, Chromebooks and some Fitbit devices can be supervised. Google notes that iPhones can't be.

Best for: parents of younger kids with Android phones who want location plus screen time controls. It isn't a fit for adult family members.

Glympse

What it is. Glympse focuses on temporary location sharing. You set a timer, send a link, and your location stops sharing when the time runs out. Viewers don't need the app. Today much of Glympse's business serves companies, for things like delivery and technician arrival updates.

Notifications. Glympse's family use is built around sharing for a set time, not ongoing alerts.

Privacy and ads. Glympse's privacy policy says it may work with a third party to show ads in its apps. Its App Store listing lists usage data used for third-party advertising. The same policy says Glympse has not sold personal information under California's legal definition, though it may share aggregate information.

Platforms. iPhone and Android.

Best for: sharing your location for one drive, trip or night out, without an always-on connection.

Looop

What it is. Looop is our app, so read this section with that in mind. Looop is a safety notifications app, not a tracking app. It doesn't ask you to check a map. It tells you when it might be worth checking in.

Notifications. Looop gives you a heads-up when severe weather is heading toward a loved one, when crime is reported near them in supported cities, and when their day looks unusual compared to their own routine. When all is well, it stays quiet. You choose which alerts you get for each person.

Independence. Loved ones join by invitation. Wellness snapshots like steps, heart rate and sleep are optional and shared only if each person chooses. There's a family map for when you need it, for example after a nudge to check in. It's never the point.

Privacy and ads. Looop never sells your data and doesn't work with advertisers.

Platforms. iPhone. That's a real limit if anyone in your family uses Android.

Best for: adults who care from a distance, like grown kids with parents in another state or parents of college students, who want to know when to call without hovering.

Which one is right for you?

Start with the people on the other end. What would they agree to, and what would they be glad to have?

  • Young kids with Android phones: Google Family Link.
  • An all-iPhone family that wants simple sharing: Apple Find My.
  • A mix of iPhone and Android: Google Maps location sharing.
  • Sharing for one trip or night out: Glympse.
  • A detailed shared map, and you're fine with an ad-supported model: Life360.
  • Grown-up family you'd like to hear about only when something seems off: Looop.

Whatever you choose, talk first. Show each person what's shared, and let them say no. A tool someone agreed to is a tool they keep.
